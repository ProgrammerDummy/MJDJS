2. Dispatched job lost in transit

Problem: request_work records the job as assigned before the response reaches the worker. If the response is lost, the job is stuck forever on a healthy worker and takes up one of its slots. With 1 slot, the worker sits idle permanently.

Your solution:

HeartbeatPing lists the (job ID, token) pairs the worker is actually running.
assigned_jobs becomes a HashMap<Uuid, AssignedJob { token, misses }>.
A job missing from one ping is tolerated; missing from the next, it’s reclaimed.
Reclaiming also sends a revoke directive with the job ID and token over HeartbeatControl, and a revoked job doesn’t report.

Why:

The server periodically compares what it thinks is assigned with what’s actually running, so lost messages heal on their own. That’s level-triggered rather than edge-triggered.
It keeps leasing per worker and uses the channel you already have.
Counting pings avoids comparing timestamps from two different machines’ clocks.
One miss is enough: the heartbeat and dispatch use separate connections and the interval is 5s, so at most one ping can legitimately miss a new job.
Keep token_map.remove after report_with_retry returns. Otherwise a ping sent while a success report is still in flight would count a miss for a job that already succeeded.




3. Report carries the wrong identity; re-registration cancel reported as Cancelled

Problem: run_job reads the current worker ID when it reports, so a stale run can report under W′. A cancel caused by re-registration also reports Cancelled, which is final and could abandon a job that’s running elsewhere.

Your solution:

Pass the worker ID from dispatch time into run_job.
Add a registration generation (AtomicU64, SeqCst). main.rs increments it before cancelling tokens.
run_job records the generation at dispatch and checks it after execution. If it changed, skip the report but still remove the job from token_map.
Keep cancelling jobs on re-registration.
A user cancel from cancel_job doesn’t increment the generation, so it still reports Cancelled.
The needs_reregister signal carries the generation the sender observed, and main.rs ignores stale signals. That fixes the double re-registration.

Why:

A result must carry the identity it was dispatched under.
Cancelled is final and means a user cancelled the job. A lost lease should report nothing and let the scheduler reclaim the job.
Incrementing before cancelling means any job that sees its token cancelled is guaranteed to also see the new generation.
Proto changes across all three
Job.dispatch_token
JobResult.dispatch_token
a list of running jobs (ID + token) on HeartbeatPing
a cancel directive on HeartbeatControl that carries the job ID, the token, and whether it’s a user cancel or a revoke
